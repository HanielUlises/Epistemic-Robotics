RF-05 · AWS small warehouse · ROS 2 · Gazebo

A link that actually falls

Every fleet demonstration on this site models restricted connectivity the same way: an agent is oblivious to an announcement because the domain declares it oblivious. The declaration is static, it is made before the run, and no link ever falls. This page reports the first run in which one does. A monitor owns the state of the radio between two robots and cuts it for a configured window; a gate stops relaying and counts what it dropped; the belief one robot holds about where the other is keeps being propagated with a covariance that grows; and at reconnection the map reconciliation of the collaborative SLAM layer is handed, for the first time, two maps that two robots actually built.

Fleet, world and per-robot SLAM from warehouse_demo, included unmodified. Reconciliation from epistemic_slam. The outage, the belief engine and the measurement are epistemic_comm, new here.

The starting point

A requirement with no code behind it

The requirement this page closes against is RF-05: on losing the link at tdisc, a robot propagates its belief about its partner's pose forward under the motion model of the last shared plan, with the covariance growing without bound, and the estimation error is to stay within vmax·Δt + σprop. It names two topics and an engine. Before this work, none of the three existed as code.

The last row is the whole difficulty in one line. link_up is in this repository, in scenarios/warehouse/snapshots/*.json, as labels: {"w0": ["link_up"]}: a proposition that a world makes true, fixed when the problem was written. What existed of connectivity was exactly the static declaration that the limitation identifies, and nothing else.

The announcement channel does not carry belief

The obvious fault injector subscribes to the channel two robots speak on and stops forwarding. It would have measured nothing, and finding out why changed the design.

Announcer::say() publishes an utterance on /eplansys/channel/private/<listener>. The only subscriber is radio, which keeps a transcript so that who was addressed is a matter of record. eplansys contains no reference to that channel at all. The epistemic model advances through a service, epistemic_state/apply_action, which the executor calls when an action completes; in the speech-act performer the two lines are speak(); conclude(true, ...), in that order. Cutting the channel empties the transcript and leaves the product update, the designated set and the goal check exactly as they were.

The channel is an audit path, not a transport. That is a reasonable thing for it to be, and it is worth knowing before building an experiment on the assumption that it is the other thing.

The apparatus

Five nodes, and where each one cuts

nodewhat it does
comm_monitor Owns the link state and is the only thing that does. Publishes /comm_monitor/link_down and /link_up, latched, at the configured instants, and answers a service for the state now.
link_gate The injector. A type-erased relay over rclcpp's generic publisher and subscription, so one node gates an odometry and an occupancy grid without a case for either. Holds no timer: it subscribes to the monitor like everything else.
belief_update RF-05's engine. Holds the last pose while the link is up; integrates the last twist forward while it is down, growing the covariance. Reads the gated odometry, so the outage is real for it.
grid_align Places two SLAM maps on the union of their extents at their common resolution. Whole-cell translation, no interpolation.
link_experiment Measures. Reads the ungated odometry, because the error to measure is against where the partner actually went and not against the silence the believer was reasoning from.

The gate is installed alongside rather than in the middle: it reads the topic the fleet already publishes and writes a second name that only the believing side subscribes to. Nothing that exists is remapped, rebuilt or told that a gate is present, and warehouse_demo is included by the scenario launch unmodified.

The outage is real, and the count is the evidence

A gate that reports nothing dropped was gating a topic nobody publishes on, and that is a launch error which otherwise looks exactly like a successful run. Over the sixty-second window the odometry gate dropped 1 765 messages having passed 1 179 before, and the map gate dropped 61 having passed 41. The belief engine also refuses to accept a partner pose while it believes the link is down, and says so, for the same reason: an arrival at that moment would mean the gate is on a different topic from the one being read.

The measurement

RF-05's criterion over three outage lengths

Three runs, differing only in how long the link stays down. The partner drives a fixed square throughout and knows nothing about the outage. The bound is vmax·Δt + σprop with vmax = 0.22 m/s, taken from the max_speed_xy the fleet's own controller enforces, and σprop = 0.05 m.

The criterion is met in all three, and the covariance invariant behaves as specified: the trace grows without decreasing and crosses σmax² = 1.0 m² at fifty seconds, at which point the engine says that where the partner is should no longer be believed.

The bound is loosest exactly where the estimate is worst

The tightest margin in every run is at t = 0, not at the end. At the instant of the cut the bound is σprop alone, 0.05 m, and the error is a few millimetres of odometry noise. From there the bound grows at 0.22 m/s while the propagated estimate tracks the partner and the error grows far more slowly: at sixty seconds the bound stands at 13.2 m against an error of 1.4 m.

So a pass is weak evidence. The bound admits any displacement a robot at full speed could produce, and a propagation that simply holds the last velocity cannot exceed it while the partner keeps its course. The unit tests state the other half: a partner that reverses at full speed the instant the link falls separates from the belief at 2v·Δt and breaches the bound for every Δt past σprop/vmax. What these three runs establish is that the engine tracks a partner that holds its course, which is what they were able to establish; they do not establish that the bound cannot be breached.

The reconciliation

The first real pair of maps was refused

The collaborative SLAM layer classifies cells, computes per-robot coverage, detects contradictions and reconciles. It passes 43 tests, and every one of them builds both grids in memory. A test that builds both grids builds them the same shape.

Two maps that two robots actually built are never the same shape. Each grows as its own robot discovers floor, and the first pair this reconciliation was ever handed came back grids differ in size: 118x128 and 134x86. The refusal is correct and is documented: a wrong registration produces a merged map that is confidently wrong, so two maps whose discretisation does not coincide are declined rather than approximated. What was missing was the step before it.

grid_align places both maps on the union of their extents at their common resolution. It is a translation by whole cells: no interpolation, no rotation, no scaling, and a cell keeps the value it had. Maps at different resolutions are still refused, because putting those on one grid would be the approximation the layer declines to make. In the reported runs the alignment is 117×128 and 133×86 onto 134×397 at 0.050 m per cell, growing as both maps grow.

Each robot learns from the other almost exactly the count the other had decided, and nothing is in conflict. That is not the reconciliation being clever; it is what a disjoint pair of observations looks like. The believing robot sits at its dock and maps what it can see from there, the partner patrols elsewhere, and the two sets of observed cells barely touch. A conflict needs two robots to have looked at the same cell and disagreed about whether it is passable, and this pair never did.

The zeros are therefore a property of this trajectory, not a result about the fusion. Producing a genuine contradiction needs the two robots routed through the same aisle from opposite ends, and that is the next run rather than this one.

Defects

Three found by integration, none in the new code

whathow it presented
Stale ABI in the fleet demo Every action node died at startup with undefined symbol: plansys2::ActionExecutorClient::finish(bool, float, string const&). The installed binaries were built when finish took three arguments; it now takes four, the fourth being the outcome the epistemic extension needs. A rebuild fixes it, and nothing warns that the installed tree is older than the library it links.
The fleet cannot drive with two robots r1's global costmap never receives its map, so it keeps the default 5×5 m extent at the origin, reports the robot out of bounds and nav2 can never plan. The topic exists with one publisher and five subscribers and the costmap's map_topic is set to it. Not diagnosed further here; the scenario drives the partner directly instead.
The measurement recounted what it should have read The first version of the reconciliation record differenced cell counts it had subscribed to itself, and reported a merged map with fewer decided cells than one of its inputs. The aligner keeps publishing as both maps grow, so by the time a reply arrives the recorder may hold a newer pair than the fusion was given. Differencing two pairs that are not the same pair is not a measurement. The counts now come from the fusion's own reply.

Scope

What this establishes

That a communication outage can be injected into a running fleet without modifying the fleet, and that the cut is real and counted. That RF-05's engine exists, propagates, grows its covariance without decreasing, and reaches the threshold past which it declares its own estimate unfit to be believed. That its acceptance criterion is met over outages of thirty, sixty and a hundred and twenty seconds against a partner in motion. And that the reconciliation of the collaborative SLAM layer runs on maps two robots built from two lasers in a simulator, which needed one step the layer did not have.

What it does not

It does not establish that the epistemic model diverges under partition, because there is nothing for it to diverge in. The bringup runs one planner and one epistemic state for the whole fleet, so an agent's accessibility relation is a column of a shared structure rather than a perspective held on a robot. RF-05 speaks of one robot's belief about another; what is measured here is a metric estimate of a pose, published alongside that structure and not inside it. Partitioning the model is a different piece of work and a larger one.

It does not establish that the goal formula still holds at reconnection by the validator's account, for the same reason: there is one model, it never diverged, and a check against it would be a check that nothing was partitioned.

And it does not establish anything about the bound being a good bound. Three runs met it; the arithmetic says a partner that reverses at full speed breaches it within a quarter of a second of the cut.

Reproduction

Running it

colcon build --packages-select epistemic_msgs epistemic_comm epistemic_slam
colcon test --packages-select epistemic_comm      # 49 tests

ros2 launch epistemic_comm link_outage_launch.py \
     gui:=false t_disc:=40 t_recon:=100 scenario:=baseline

t_disc and t_recon are seconds from the monitor's first clock tick, and the schedule is open loop on purpose. An outage triggered by distance would make its own duration a function of the trajectory it perturbs, and the measurement could not then separate the two. Two JSON records are written per run, one for the belief error against the bound and one for the reconciliation. The six this page reports are published beside it: short, baseline and long carry every sample of the error and the bound, and the three reconcile records carry what each robot learned. Every figure in the tables above is read out of them.
