The watcher

A carrier must take its load into the bay its order names, let an ally in a booth know which bay that is, and not let a watcher know. The watcher stands at one of two posts, west or east; it knows which, and the others do not. It sees what happens on its own side of the hall and nothing on the other. Every way of telling the ally is on a side: the gate of each bay, which the booth sees, is on that bay's side, and so is each of the booth's two windows, where the carrier can show a lamp.

The goal is \(\mathit{in\text{-}bay} \wedge \mathit{Kw}_{r2}\,\mathit{dest}\text{-}x \wedge \neg\mathit{Kw}_{r3}\,\mathit{dest}\text{-}x\). A classical planner cannot state its last conjunct, and no plan that does not branch meets the goal in more than two of the four worlds. The epistemic policy reads a duty board that names the watcher's post, which nothing in the goal mentions, and then tells the ally on the side the watcher is not.

The policy met the goal in 39 of 40 runs, and the watcher's laser had the carrier in view 0 times. The direct classical carrier met it in 20, the discreet one in 17; between them they gave the bay away in 40 runs, and their executors reported 37 of those as a success. Judged by sensors, not by the planner: each run is scored by what the ally's and the watcher's own lasers recorded. 40 seeds, ten per world, each run under all three formulations from the same start.

Package watcher_demo; policy from eplansys, search by Aletheia, classical plans by POPF[4] through PlanSys2[5]. Full account in The Watcher (PDF): the domain, the proof that no sequence works, the classical formulations, the batch and the defects found on the way.

Environment

Environment

The hall is 14 by 7.6 m. From west to east: bay X, the west hall, a solid block with the ally's booth in it, the east hall, bay Y. Each bay opens onto its hall by a gate and onto the south corridor by a door. The booth has a window onto each hall. Each hall has a watcher's post near its north wall and an entry from the corridor, under which a baffle stands so that nothing of the corridor can be seen from the post. The carrier starts in the corridor before two boards: the order board, whose lit slot names the bay, and the duty board, whose lit slot names the watcher's post.

The actions are event models of dynamic epistemic logic[1], planned over as in[2]. Four worlds, one per order and post, all designated; the watcher knows its post, nobody knows the order. What the watcher observes is decided world by world by an observability condition on its post, in EPDDL (?w (if (west ?w) Fully else Oblivious)) for the west window; an oblivious watcher believes nothing happened. The ontic type with a null event is the domain's own library, sightlines.epddl. The drive to a window is declared public, which is more than the watcher sees: the declaration is cautious, and the runs are judged by sensors, not by it.

No plan, one policy

Why no sequence works, and what the policy does

No sequence meets the goal in more than two of the four worlds. The ally comes to know the bay only from an action it observes fully whose events name the bay: gate, say-w or say-e. A sequence contains its actions in every world, and each of the three is observed fully by the watcher in two of the four: the gate where the watcher stands on the bay's side, each window where it stands on that window's side. And knowledge of a fact no action changes is never lost under the product update, since an edge of the updated model needs an edge of the old one. So every informing action a sequence contains loses two worlds, and a sequence with none informs nobody. Aletheia's GBFS, which searches for one sequence for all four worlds, exhausts.

Where the watcher stands on the bay's side it tells the ally at the far window and goes in along the corridor; elsewhere it takes the gate. Classical planning

Classical planning, with every action the epistemic domain has

The classical model has the same actions, each lasting the time its route takes: reading the order and the duty board, the drive through a gate, the drives to a window, the lamp, the back route. Telling the ally is a fact a gate or a lamp makes true. What it cannot have is a goal that a third robot not know: a goal over facts can at most forbid an action, and which one to forbid depends on where the watcher stands. The carrier reads its order and plans its delivery with POPF.

  • Direct. POPF takes the gate, 61.6 s against 114.8 s for the shortest window plan. It gives the bay away wherever the watcher stands on the bay's side.
  • Discreet. The modeller, who knows a watcher exists and cannot say where, rules the gates out. POPF takes the east window for either bay, and gives the bay away wherever the watcher stands east.

Neither reads the duty board: no goal a classical planner is given needs it, and replanning would not change that, since the plan has nothing to learn from it. Neither fails loudly: in the worlds it gets wrong it still reaches its bay and informs the ally, and its executor reports success.

Who saw what

Judged by the robots' own lasers

On the ally and on the watcher runs an observer node that the planner and the executor tell nothing (src/observer_node.cpp). It reads its robot's laser, places the returns in the world with the robot's own odometry, and records when the carrier was in view in each region: the gates, the window spots, the halls, the bays. The lamp counts as seen only while the laser has the carrier at a window. From that record each observer names a bay, or nothing. A run meets the goal when the carrier ended in its bay, the ally named that bay, and the watcher named none. For the policy, the executor's model, asked at the end whether \(\mathit{Kw}_{r2}\) and \(\mathit{Kw}_{r3}\) hold, agreed with both observers in 40 of 40 runs.

Against a watcher that knows the policy. The product update says what the watcher knows from the events it observes; a watcher that also knew the policy could ask which of the policy's runs fit what it saw. The policy keeps the secret from it if, in any two worlds the watcher cannot tell apart at the start and that differ on the bay, it shows the watcher the same observations. tools/opacity.py walks the policy in every world and compares. As declared it is not opaque: with the watcher west, the world of bay X shows it the drive to the east window, declared public, and the world of bay Y does not. Those declarations are more than the watcher sees. Declared by the sightlines instead (watcher-sightlines.epddl: what the boards say seen only by the carrier, the back route by nobody, a window only from its side), Aletheia returns the same policy in the same 68 expansions, with no agent's beliefs collapsing on the way, and in all four worlds the watcher observes only that the carrier read the boards: opaque, as long as the watcher stays at its post. A first version of that file declared the boards' reading unseen by the ally as well; the ally then held the lamp impossible, its beliefs collapsed, and its knowledge held only vacuously. Planning with --consistent-beliefs catches it, and every check here now does.

Found on the way

Defects found, and a batch discarded

  • The executor's knowledge requirements. ePlanSys's executor checks the modal conjuncts of all of an action's designated events together, as one conjunction. An action whose events need \(K_{r1}\,\mathit{dest}\text{-}x\) in one and \(K_{r1}\,\neg\mathit{dest}\text{-}x\) in the other can never be dispatched, and the first runs failed at the lamp. Every such event now asks \(\mathit{Kw}_{r1}\,\mathit{dest}\text{-}x\) beside its own fact, which says the same, as the gate signal's domain does. The check should be the disjunction over events.
  • A library named like its domain. plank stops with an unknown exception when a domain and one of its action-type libraries share a name; the library is called sightlines.
  • A label YAML reads as true. A sensing node's label y, written to a parameter file, came back a boolean and the node aborted.
  • A dangling reference, and a batch discarded. The drive node iterated over the string array of a parameter object already destroyed. In a first batch of 120 runs it crashed the carrier's drive in 17, 17 of them in the policy's gate branch; the carrier, its last velocity still applied, then wandered until the time limit. That batch is kept apart as discarded, and every figure on this page is from the second, run on the fixed node.

Limits

Limitations

  • A watcher at its post. Against a watcher that knows the policy and stays where it is, the policy is opaque under the sightlines. A watcher that moves, choosing where to look from what it has seen, is not modelled.
  • A narrow pocket. 7 of the 120 runs ended at the time limit with the carrier stalled at the east baffle, in or just past the pocket between it and the corridor wall, 0.6 m tall: 1 of the policy's, 0 of the direct carrier's and 6 of the discreet carrier's, which takes that route every time. They count against the goal, and they are the controller's, not the planner's.
  • The duty board. The carrier learns the watcher's post by reading a duty board, a sensing action like the order board, whose outcome its laser decides. Where the watcher stands is given to the carrier by the hall.
  • Timing. A watcher that timed the mission could tell the window branch, which is longer, from the gate branch; the domain has no durations.
  • The classical side is a classical planner given the task's facts. Compilations of multi-agent epistemic planning into classical planning[3] represent what each agent believes as facts of their own, and could state the goal; they are epistemic planning in another form, and are not compared here.
