The watcher that moves

The carrier of the watcher must still take its load into the bay its order names, let the ally know which, and not let the watcher know. Now the watcher may change posts, through a passage behind the north wall, out of everyone's sight, while the carrier drives; and it may know the policy. What the carrier read on the duty board can be out of date by the time it reaches a window, and a watcher that knows the policy reads the carrier's choices, not only its signals.

Two things had to change in the planning, and both are general. The planner's policies branched on every designated event, so after a drive they branched on whether the watcher had moved, which the carrier cannot see: policies no robot can follow. Branching instead on what each action's actor observes, the planner finds the carrier looking down the hall before it tells, and changing windows when the watcher has come. And a policy correct under the product update can still give the order away to a watcher that knows the policy; whether it does is checked against the hall's sightlines.

Against the watcher's best strategy, the opaque policy kept the order in 20 of 20 runs, judged by the robots' own lasers and by a watcher that knows the policy. The published policy kept it in 0, the shortest policy in 0, the classical carriers in 0 and 10. 20 seeds, five per world, each under five formulations and two watcher strategies from the same start.

Package watcher_moves_demo; domain epddl-workspace/watcher-mobile; Aletheia with subjective branching on its branch feature/subjective-branching, and ePlanSys's executor and planner plugin built on it in an overlay. Full account in The Watcher That Moves (PDF).

Environment

The hall and the domain

The domain (make_domain.py, for any number K of changes of post) has the carrier at one of five places, its start, the two windows and the two gate approaches. Every drive between them is an action with one event per thing the watcher may do while it lasts, stay or go to the other post; only the watcher tells them apart. The carrier can look down the hall it stands in. Observability follows the sightlines, except that the watcher is taken to see every drive, the cautious reading. Every plan is made with --consistent-beliefs.

Subjective branching

Policies a robot can follow

AO* over a multi-pointed model branches after an action on its designated events, one branch each. After a drive that is one branch for "the watcher stayed" and one for "it moved", and the carrier cannot tell which it is on. The change to Aletheia branches after an action on the classes of designated worlds its actor cannot tell apart: where the actor observes every event fully, nothing changes; where it does not, the unseen outcome stays as uncertainty in the state. --subjective auto takes the actor of a ground action to be the first agent its name gives. Without the option the planner returned what the installed one does on 28 of its 28 benchmarks; with it, 24 of the 24 tasks of the workspace's other domains have the policies they had, same depth and leaves.

Under subjective branching the policy is longer, depth 2K+5 against K+5, and that is the point: one look for each change of post the watcher may have made. For K = 1 without the gates: read the order and the duty board, drive to the window on the side the watcher is not, look. Clear: show the lamp, go in by the back route. The watcher has come: drive to the other window and show the lamp there without looking again, since the watcher has used its move.

Opacity

A secret from a watcher that knows the policy

A policy is opaque to the watcher if any two worlds it cannot tell apart at the start, and that differ on the order, produce the same set of traces of what it sees, over every choice it makes. tools/opacity_sightlines.py computes the traces from the hall rather than from the domain's declarations. The shortest policy is not opaque, for any K tried. With K = 0 it does not even read the duty board: it drives to the window on the side opposite its bay and looks, and crosses to its bay's gate if the watcher is there. Under the product update that is sound, since a carrier at a window says nothing about the bay; a watcher that knows the policy reads it as it is. Without the gates the carrier's visible choices depend on where the watcher is and never on the order, and the policy is opaque for every K. The planner does not search for opacity: it was found by removing the gates and checking.

Evaluation

Paired evaluation over 20 seeds

Five formulations: the published policy (v1), the shortest policy with the gates, the opaque one, both planned with K = 1 and subjective branching, and the classical direct and discreet carriers. Two watcher strategies: stay, and chase, which knows that a carrier that reads the duty board tells on the side the watcher is not, and leaves for the other post the moment the carrier starts to drive. The watcher is judged by the events, as before, and by the policy: it names the bay of every other run of the same formulation and strategy, from the same post, that showed it the same trace of places and lamp, if they all had one bay. A run hides the order when the carrier delivered, the ally named the bay, and the watcher named none either way.

On the way

Changes to the executor and to the hall

ePlanSys's executor keeps its own model of the mission and applies to it the outcome of every action. A drive whose outcome the carrier cannot see has no outcome to report, and the executor asked for one. Its state node and its planner plugin were therefore built over the changed Aletheia, each taking a parameter for subjective branching, so that an outcome the actor cannot see is kept in the model as uncertainty. Both are built in an overlay workspace over the installed ePlanSys, which they leave as it was.

The baffles that hide the corridor from the posts left a pocket 0.6 m tall between each baffle and the corridor wall, in which the carrier's controller sometimes oscillated; crossing between the windows goes through both pockets. The baffles were lowered to leave 0.85 m, the sightlines were checked again, and the batch was begun again. The 20 runs of the first attempt, 1 of which stalled, are kept apart as discarded.

The sightlines variant of the watcher hid the reading of the boards from the ally, whose knowledge of the order then held only because its beliefs had collapsed. That variant was corrected and republished, and every plan on this page is made with --consistent-beliefs, which refuses such states.

Limits

Limitations

The runs give the watcher one change of post; the planning study goes to three. The domain assumes that the watcher moves only while the carrier drives and reaches its new post first, and the hall is built so that it does: in the runs it arrived first in 60 of 60 moves. Opacity is checked rather than planned for: the opaque policy comes from a domain without the gates, and a planner that searched for opacity would have to compare the runs of two worlds as it searched. The watcher that knows the policy learns it from the other runs of the batch, five per world and strategy.
