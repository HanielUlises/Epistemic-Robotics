The collapsed wing

Three robots enter a building nobody has mapped. A victim lies in its north wing, which the rest of the building reaches by three ways: a west doorway, an east doorway, and between them a passage that winds through collapsed rooms, the longest way and one no single spot sees in full. Rubble may block any of the three, and at least one is clear. Two scouts come in by the side entrances and can each reach one doorway; a medic waits in the entrance hall. Every robot builds its own map with slam_toolbox from its own laser, and a scout decides its doorway on that map.

The goal is \(\mathit{with\text{-}victim}(m)\), and the medic may cross into the north wing only by a way it knows to be clear: each crossing requires \(K_m\,\mathit{clear}\), not \(\mathit{clear}\). The epistemic policy sends both scouts out at once and has them say over the radio what they found. When both doorways turn out blocked, the second scout announces the middle passage clear without anyone having seen it, from what it saw, what it heard and the fact that one way is clear, and the medic crosses it.

The policy brought the medic to the victim in 21 of 21 runs, never into a blocked way, in 156.1 s on average and 229.1 s at worst. A classical plan that assumes every way clear reached the victim in 21 runs, after entering 12 blocked ways in 9 of them, in 188.3 s on average and 434.4 s at worst. A classical plan that gates the crossing on a scout's check, the knowledge precondition written as a fact, reached it in 18, and in the 3 runs where only the middle passage was clear it had no plan. Judged by where the medic ended, by its own odometry, and every doorway decision by the world. 21 seeds, three per world, each run under all three formulations from the same start.

Package collapsed_wing_demo; policy from eplansys, search by Aletheia, classical plans by POPF[4] through PlanSys2[5], maps by slam_toolbox[6]. Full account in The Collapsed Wing (PDF): the domain, the policy in each world, the classical formulations, the batch and the implementation defects.

Environment

Environment

The building is 20 by 14 m. Its south half holds the entrance hall, where the medic starts, between a west wing and an east wing, which the scouts s2 and s1 enter by the side entrances. A wall closes off the north wing, where the victim lies. The west and east doorways cross it from the wings. Between them, from the north side of the hall, a passage winds through collapsed rooms in three long legs, 21 m in all; it enters from the hall at its east end and leaves into the north wing at its west end. The medic's way to the victim is 23 m by either doorway and 31 m by the passage. Desks, columns and debris stand in the wings, the hall and the north wing, off every route.

Each seed takes one of the seven worlds in turn, the ways clear or blocked with at least one clear, and perturbs every robot's starting pose. Every robot runs its own slam_toolbox, in its own namespace and its own map frame, from its own laser; no map is shared. A scout's map decides a doorway when its cells in the outlined region say so: blocked when at least 6 of them are known occupied, clear when none is and at least 90 % of its 10 cm blocks hold a known free cell, by slam_toolbox's own thresholds. The rays of the building are cast in every world before any run (tools/check_views.py): a scout at its vantage decides its own doorway, and decides it right, and does not decide the other; no spot a robot stops at has the passage's rubble in sight.

The actions are event models of dynamic epistemic logic[1], planned over as in[2]. A scout can reach only its own doorway, s1 the east and s2 the west, and no action looks at the middle passage. A message has one event per value, each requiring that the speaker know whether the way is clear beside the fact itself. Each crossing requires that the medic know the way is clear. The initial model has seven worlds, all designated: each way is clear or blocked, the disjunction that at least one is clear is common knowledge, and no robot knows more.

The policy

One policy for seven worlds

Aletheia's AO*[3] returns, at depth 7 after 424 expansions, a policy with three leaves: 

goto-e(s1) · goto-w(s2) · inspect-e(s1)
  on e-inspect-e-clear: tell-e(s1) · cross-e(m)
  on e-inspect-e-blocked: tell-e(s1) · inspect-w(s2)
    on e-inspect-w-clear: tell-w(s2) · cross-w(m)
    on e-inspect-w-blocked: tell-m(s2) · cross-m(m)

The two drives come first and are dispatched together, so that both scouts reach their doorways while the first is still to be decided. s1's message comes before s2 looks, in the branch where the east doorway is blocked, because it is what lets s2 conclude in the last branch: having heard the east doorway blocked and seen the west one blocked, s2 knows the middle passage clear, since one way is. Its message makes that knowledge the medic's. No branch has the medic cross a way it has not been told is clear, and in none does a robot enter the passage before the medic does.

Classical planning

Two classical formulations

A classical planner cannot state that the medic must know the way, nor represent what a scout has seen. Two formulations take the strongest positions open to it. In the first, optimistic, every way is assumed clear, and POPF[4] sends the medic by the shortest. When the medic's own laser finds rubble on its route, its drive stops and fails; the mission removes that way's assumption from the problem expert's state, places the medic in the way, and replans, which backs it out to the hall and sends it by another. The scouts are not used. In the second, gated, the medic's crossing of a way requires a fact, (checked_clear_<way>), that only a scout's check of that doorway gives: the knowledge precondition written as a fact. A check is planned as if it will find the doorway clear, and when the scout's map decides rubble the mission replans. No check of the middle passage exists, so where it alone is clear there is no plan, unless the modeller writes the conclusion by elimination into the domain by hand, for this building.

Measurement

What a run is judged by

A run reaches the victim when the medic ends within 0.5 m of it, by its own odometry. It enters a dead end when the medic sets out on a way and its laser finds rubble on it. A crossing is told when the way was said clear on the radio before the medic set out on it. Every change of a scout's doorway decision is checked against the world. Mission time is measured in simulated seconds from a common start; the distance each robot drove and the area of the building its own map holds at the end are recorded beside it.

The policy never sent the medic into a blocked way, and every way it crossed had been said clear on the radio before it set out. Assuming every way clear, the optimistic medic took the west doorway first; where it was clear it reached the victim 35.6 s sooner than the policy on average, having not waited for a scout. Where it was blocked the medic found the rubble with its own laser, backed out and tried the next way, and these 9 runs took 122.5 s longer than the policy's on average. Paired by seed, the policy was faster in 9 of 21 seeds, and its worst time was 229.1 s against 434.4 s.

The gated formulation follows the policy's order but not its schedule. Where the east doorway was clear the two coincide, within 0.5 s on average. Where it was blocked and the west was clear, the gated plan sends s2 only after s1's check has failed, and arrived 35.5 s after the policy, whose s2 had driven out at the start. Where only the middle passage was clear it had no plan, in all 3 runs.

Every robot's own SLAM map held, at the end, 51, 44 and 152 m² of the building on average for s1, s2 and the medic. No scout's map decided a doorway wrongly in any run.

Implementation

Implementation defects

One defect was found while the demonstration was built. ePlanSys's executor dispatches together consecutive actions of a policy whose vocabulary actions neither write what the other reads. With each vocabulary action touching only its own robot's fact, the medic's crossing was dispatched beside the message it waits for, and the knowledge check stopped it before it moved. The vocabulary now carries a fact for the radio, which every message writes and every crossing reads, so that a crossing is never dispatched before the message that makes it known; the scouts' drives and inspections leave it alone and are still dispatched together.

Limits

Limitations

The results hold under several restrictions of the model. Aletheia's AO* minimises the depth of the worst branch and has no durations: it orders the scouts' looks by depth, so the medic goes by the east doorway whenever it is clear, although the west is as long, and it would not prefer a shorter way if one existed. The radio reaches every robot and never fails; the project's link demonstrations treat messages that may be lost. That at least one way is clear is common knowledge from the start, and is what makes the conclusion by elimination sound: a building in which every way might be blocked would leave the last branch without a guarantee, and the policy without the passage.

The experiment has restrictions of its own. The drives follow routes on a roadmap, steered by a dynamic window on each robot's own laser, and not a planner over the SLAM map; the maps decide what is known, not where a robot drives. The robots' poses come from the simulator's odometry, so that a map's frame is the world's up to slam_toolbox's own corrections. Mission time includes the planners' wall time, during which the simulation advanced, and the batch ran beside other simulations at a real-time factor near 0.4.
