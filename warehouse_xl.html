Open-RMF · 30 × 50 m · ROS 2

A larger warehouse floor

The warehouse the bridge had been driving is fourteen metres by twenty-one, with two aisles and twelve waypoints: enough to show the pipeline works and not enough to show it scaling. This floor is thirty by fifty with thirty-four aisles, a service lane running the length of the building, and three robots under Open-RMF. Every aisle opens onto the lane and onto nothing else, so an aisle cannot be seen into until a robot stands at its mouth, which is the property the whole approach rests on.

Floor, navigation graph and building map generated from one source in warehouse_xl_rmf_demo. Dispatched by eplansys-rmf, driven by Open-RMF over Gazebo Classic.

The floor

There was nothing to download

No large warehouse map exists for Open-RMF. Measured against their own meshes rather than their descriptions, the candidates on Gazebo Fuel each fail in a different way, and the failures are not visible from the catalogue page.

CandidateFootprintCollision geometry
OpenRobotics/Depot 29 × 15 m A ground plane, and nothing else
OpenRobotics/Warehouse 30 × 50 m Shell and pillars: 1.2 % occupied at robot height
industrial-warehouse = AWS small Complete, and no larger than what we had

Depot is the model usually reached for, and it is the trap: its racks and walls are visuals, a laser passes through them, and a floor plan rasterised from its collision meshes comes back empty. The largest maps in rmf_demos, the airport terminal and the campus, are not warehouses.

So the hall is the Fuel model, whose collision mesh really is the building, and the shelving inside it is AWS RoboMaker's, whose racks carry collision meshes a laser can see. The arrangement — eighteen rack rows either side of a central lane, clear of the ten pillars — is ours.

How it is built

One definition, three artefacts

The world, the navigation graph and the building map are generated from tools/layout.py. None is edited by hand, so none can drift from the others, and the hall's own geometry is read off its collision mesh rather than copied from its description. 

python3 tools/make_world.py        --out worlds/warehouse_xl.world
python3 tools/make_nav_graph.py    --out maps/nav_graphs/0.yaml
python3 tools/make_building_map.py --nav-graph maps/nav_graphs/0.yaml \
                                   --out maps/warehouse_xl.building.yaml

Every waypoint and every lane is checked against the floor before it is written. The check is not decoration: it refused three layouts that looked right on paper — aisle ends that met the pillar row, a cross aisle that ran through the last row of shelving once the row count changed, and two robots nominating a single charger, a waypoint holding exactly one robot.

The fourth it did not refuse, and that one is worth recording. A pallet jack placed at the south dock purely for the look of the thing overlapped the lane out of the east charger by twenty centimetres. The check passed, because it knew about racks and pillars and not about props; RMF reported the task underway; and the robot drove out of its charger, pressed into the pallet jack and stopped, with nothing anywhere reporting a collision. A thing that stands on the floor is an obstacle whatever it was put there for. The props are part of the floor definition now, and the same check that missed it rejects it.

The run

Measured, not asserted

The mission below is the survey domain of eplansys, unchanged, executed by three TurtleBot3 Waffles over Open-RMF. Gazebo is on the left and RViz on the right; the captions name the step of the policy the fleet is on at that moment, and their times come from the executor's own log rather than from a stopwatch.

That an aisle can only be entered from the lane is a claim about the floor, so it is put to the fleet and answered in metres. The run is recorded from /fleet_states and drawn over the graph the robot was routing on. 

t=0s   (11.00, -23.64)  charger_east_south
t=12s  ( 9.06, -22.05)  onto the south cross aisle
t=24s  ( 3.29, -22.04)  west along it, ~0.58 m/s
t=36s  ( 0.00, -22.04)  lane_00, the foot of the service lane
t=48s  ( 0.01, -16.86)  north up the lane
t=60s  ( 0.01, -11.35)
t=72s  ( 0.00,  -5.59)
t=84s  ( 2.70,   0.25)  turned east at the mouth of aisle 8
t=96s  ( 4.19,   0.25)  east_08

A task is pinned to a named robot rather than dispatched for bidding, which is the path the epistemic bridge uses: the robot that senses is the agent the model credits with knowing. 

ros2 launch warehouse_xl_rmf_demo warehouse_xl_fleet.launch.py
ros2 run eplansys_rmf_probe submit_probe -F warehouseXL -R r1 -p east_08

What it establishes

And what it does not

The mission completes and every check passes: 

observed e-scan-dirty
relay says e-scan-dirty on /eplansys/channel/private/scout
mission complete

ok   (Kw scout contaminated) holds
ok   (Kw relay contaminated) holds
ok   (Kw observer contaminated) does not hold
ok   scout was spoken to, 1 time(s)
ok   observer was spoken to by nobody

Three formulas put to the epistemic state, and two radio transcripts read off the fleet. The last two are the ones worth having: they are facts about what was published and received rather than about the model, and they are what tells a run that honoured the private channel apart from a run that never spoke at all.

A full account of the floor, the fleet and the eight defects the integration turned up is in A Warehouse Floor at Scale (PDF).

How the schedule mediates the service lane under sustained three-robot traffic has not been measured. The lane is the one resource all thirty-four aisles share, so it is where contention would appear, and it is the next thing worth knowing about this floor.

One environment note, since it stops every rmf_demos fleet and not only this one. The fleet manager imports fastapi, and the fastapi that Jammy packages is 0.63, written against pydantic 1. A pydantic 2 installed in ~/.local shadows the apt pydantic 1.8 for every interpreter on the machine and the import fails, so no robot moves. The launch sets PYTHONNOUSERSITE=1, which restores the pair apt installed together and needs nothing installed or removed.
