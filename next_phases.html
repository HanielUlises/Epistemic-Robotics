Where the project stands

Knowledge that only grows, and belief that can be revised

Every page of this project so far rests on the same object, a Kripke model updated by the product with an event model, and on the same discipline: a robot knows a fact only when its own laser has decided it. That discipline bought a planner whose successes the map backs, and it has two prices. Knowledge only grows, and the facts a robot can decide are those a planar laser decides from where the robot stands.

The product update builds its new relation from the old one. Two new worlds are indistinguishable for a robot only if the worlds they came from already were, and the robot cannot tell the two events apart:

\[ (w,e)\; R'_i\; (v,f) \;\iff\; w\, R_i\, v \;\;\text{and}\;\; e\, Q_i\, f \]

Every edge after the update projects onto an edge before it, so the update can remove possibilities a robot entertained and never return one it had excluded1,2. A fact that stays put, once known, stays known. The same clause is how belief goes wrong. When a robot cannot tell a private event from nothing having happened, its new relation keeps only the copies in which nothing happened, and the actual world loses its own edge. The hotel incident measured that loss during a fleet mission, the reachable frame passing from S5n to KD45n. A KD45 belief that a later look contradicts loses seriality as well, and in the changing ward Aletheia, given such a belief, exhausted its search without a policy.

The project's answer keeps every relation an equivalence and puts plausibility in the valuation: each world carries its Spohn rank3 as a grade atom, belief is a formula over knowledge and those atoms, and a refuting look removes worlds as any observation does. The mixed fleet gave each robot its own ranking and weighed reports by trust; the faulty sensor guarded actions with a belief of degree two. Nothing in plank, Aletheia or ePlanSys changed for any of it.

The consequence

Because every relation stays an equivalence, every state the planner touches has a second, geometric reading. That is where the first phase starts.

Phase one · theory

The same states as simplicial complexes

What does the topology of an epistemic state prove that its graph only shows?

Builds on
belief revision, the coordinated attack, distributed knowledge
First deliverable
an export from plank's states to complexes, with each group's connectivity logged per update
Wanted
combinatorial topology, epistemic logic, Python

In a chromatic simplicial complex each vertex is the local state of one agent and carries that agent's colour, and each facet, one vertex of every colour, is a global state. Two facets are indistinguishable for an agent exactly when they share that agent's vertex. Goubault, Ledent and Rajsbaum showed that pure complexes of this kind and proper S5 Kripke models, those in which some agent tells any two distinct worlds apart, are two presentations of the same thing, and that the product update has a simplicial counterpart4,5. Within S5 nothing is gained in what can be said. What is gained is a shape, and the shape of a complex decides what a distributed protocol can and cannot achieve6.

Local states made explicit

In a Kripke model indistinguishability is given with the model. In a complex it follows from what each agent holds: a robot's vertex is its map, its readings and the messages it received, and two global states look the same to it because its vertex is in both. That is how the robots on these pages already work, and the complex would make the link between a robot's data and its place in the epistemic state something to inspect rather than a convention of the encoding.

Topology as a proof of impossibility

Common knowledge among a group holds at a facet exactly when it holds at every facet reachable through vertices of that group, so it is constant on connected components7. A goal of common knowledge is therefore a requirement on the update: it must disconnect the facets where the fact holds from those where it does not, and each level of mutual knowledge is a step of distance. The coordinated attack and its version among many robots measured what each level costs over a lossy radio. In a complex that cost becomes a distance, and the impossibility becomes the statement that no update over such a link disconnects the complex. The same reading would make a theorem of what the thesis argues in prose, that a reconciliation after an outage should restore compatibility between two robots' states and not identity.

Robots that drop out

A complex whose facets may lack a vertex is impure, and the missing vertex reads as an agent that is not there. Its logic is KB4n, in which an absent agent knows everything at the worlds where it is absent, falsehood included8. That is a reading of lost seriality that is not inconsistency: a robot that believes nothing consistent at a world is a robot with no part in it. Whether it fits is open. A robot whose link has fallen is not a crashed process; it keeps moving and observing, absent from the exchange to its teammates and present to itself.

Distributed knowledge, and states that are not proper

In knowledge no robot holds alone the room is distributed knowledge of the cleaner and the concierge: the face their two vertices form lies in a single facet. A state with two worlds no robot tells apart is not proper, and the ward has them, since the atom carrying the porter's second walk is observed by nobody. Such states need semi-simplicial sets, in which two facets may share all their vertices9.

What the complexes do not settle

The simplicial update also projects onto the complex it updates, so revision stays where the grade atoms put it. Nor is belief native here: atoms sit on vertices as local facts, and a rank is local to no robot. It would have to be carried by the facet, and whether belief over facet ranks keeps the topological results is the most interesting question of the phase. The work begins with code: an export from plank's grounded states and Aletheia's search nodes to complexes, a check of properness after every update, and each group's connectivity logged beside the existing traces, so that every demonstration on this site can be read both ways.

Phase two · perception

Perception in three dimensions

Which facts does a three-dimensional laser make decidable, from where, and at what cost?

Builds on
the collapsed wing, the faulty sensor, check_views.py
First deliverable
a Gazebo world with a simulated 3D laser, and a node that turns voxels into decided atoms
Wanted
3D perception in ROS 2, simulation, scene graphs

Today every robot builds its map with slam_toolbox from a planar laser, and a fact is decided by a rule over that laser's returns. In the ward a door is decided only from a bend inside its lobby, since from the hall a laser gets at most four returns off a door and the rule needs five, and before anything runs tools/check_views.py casts rays over the floor plan in every state of the doors to confirm the rule decides each door where the domain says it does, and decides it right.

The thesis found that an atom's logical form sets how hard it is to acquire. An occupied region is established by a single occupied cell; a free one needs every cell, and that universal statement fails on a few unobserved cells as easily as on an obstacle. A three-dimensional laser moves the boundary both ways. It makes decidable what the plane cannot see, and it makes a free region a statement about a volume, harder still to establish.

A three-dimensional map need not stay a cloud of voxels. A 3D scene graph layers it into a building, its rooms, the places a robot can stand and the objects in them, with the relations between them13,14. For this project its appeal is that those relations are already almost atoms: a bed in a bay, a door between a lobby and the hall, a patient on a bed. A decision rule then becomes a rule over the graph, and what the graph has not settled enters the epistemic state as an atom still undecided.

The phase keeps the discipline that has served the project. Each new atom needs a decision rule with three outcomes, true, false and undecided, and an undecided reading produces no event. Each rule needs its own check, a three-dimensional successor to the ray-casting script that proves on the model of the floor from which poses it decides, and that it decides correctly. Errors of the sensor itself, glass that returns nothing and dark surfaces that return late, enter as on the faulty sensor's floor, as worlds in which a sensor is unsound, ranked and diagnosed. And since what can be decided now depends on where the robot stands and at what height it looks, choosing the pose from which to observe10 becomes part of the planning problem, the line the thesis left open.

The concrete pieces are a Gazebo world with a simulated three-dimensional laser, a voxel map such as OctoMap11 beside the planar one, the node that turns voxels or graph relations into decided atoms, and a domain in which an atom only height decides changes the policy.

Phase three · language

Vision-language models as informants

How should a robot weigh an answer that is fluent, useful and sometimes wrong?

Builds on
the mixed fleet, the faulty sensor, the warehouse at scale
First deliverable
a ROS 2 node that turns a model's answer into a report event with a declared trust
Wanted
vision-language models, evaluation, ROS 2

A vision-language model answers questions about an image in open vocabulary: what the thing standing in an aisle is, whether it can be moved by hand, what a label on it says. A laser decides that something occupies the aisle; it cannot decide what. The model decides it fluently and is sometimes wrong with full confidence. In the terms of this project its answer is not an observation. It is a report from an agent whose reliability is unknown, and the project already has the machinery for reports of that kind.

An answer would enter the epistemic state as a belief report weighed by trust, as in the mixed fleet: the worlds it rules out are raised in rank, not removed, so that a later observation can still overturn it.

Whether the model's errors belong to an answer or to the model is the question the faulty sensor asked of lasers, and it matters more here: the same model asked twice about the same image is likely to be wrong the same way, so a second answer confirms nothing, as a second look by the same short-sighted scout added nothing. Knowledge preconditions stay on the atoms a laser decides, and an action that rests on what only the model has said is guarded by a belief of a stated degree.

Two further roles are worth trying, under the same rule. A model shown a scene could propose the atoms a domain should name, and a model could translate an operator's request into an EPDDL goal that plank's parser and the planner then check, so that its part is a draft the formal system accepts or rejects, never a meaning it is trusted to have understood. Both touch a problem older than these models, how a symbol stays attached to the thing it names12. The first pieces are a ROS 2 node that turns an answer into a report event with a declared trust, and a floor on which the model and the laser can disagree, so that how often, and on what, can be counted.

Contribute

Working on it together

Each phase is larger than one person, and each asks for a different kind of contributor. A domain, a floor or a counterexample is as welcome as code: several findings on this site began as a run that went wrong.

Phase 1

Topology

Someone at home in combinatorial topology or the simplicial semantics of epistemic logic, and someone to write the exporter that brings it to the states the planner already produces.

Start with: the hotel and coordinated-attack traces.

Phase 2

Perception

Experience with three-dimensional sensing in ROS 2: simulated lasers, voxel maps, scene graphs, and decision rules that can be proved on a floor plan before they run.

Start with: the ward and check_views.py.

Phase 3

Language

Work with vision-language models, and the patience to treat them as fallible witnesses whose answers are counted against the laser's.

Start with: the warehouse aisles and the mixed fleet.

Open an issue Browse eplansys

Say which phase, which question, and what you would try. The code is in eplansys, eplansys-rmf, Aletheia and Epistemic-Robotics. Work that comes of it is published here, with its runs, its logs and its report, under the names of those who did it.
